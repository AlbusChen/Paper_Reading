Distributed Agent System: Fault-Tolerant Collaboration Among Embodied Agents ★ 高度相关
Kai Yu, Lu Chen, Hanqi Li · 2026-07-12
cs.MAcs.AImulti-agent systemsfault toleranceembodied agentsagent communicationsingle-agentheterogeneous agents
The paper addresses reliability challenges in long-horizon embodied agent task execution under resource constraints and uncertain environments. It proposes Distributed Agent System, a device-edge-cloud framework for heterogeneous agents with a two-layer fault-tolerance design covering single-agent execution alignment and cross-agent communication via semi-formal language protocols. 中文摘要

Sudipto Ghosh, Tanmoy Chakraborty · 2026-07-12
cs.AIcs.CLcs.LGgated routingadaptive depthmulti-agent reasoningcommunication costmulti-agentagent communicationinter-agent communication
This paper proposes GRADE, a hierarchical multi-agent reasoning system designed to reduce the cost of multi-agent ensembling. It uses learned gates to control agent selection, hierarchy depth, inter-agent communication, and branch pruning, trained with a collaborative variant of GRPO and supported by a hot-swappable expert registry. cs.MAcs.AIllm agentssocial deductionbelief trackingmulti-agent evaluationmulti-agent
The paper studies evaluation of LLM agents in hidden-information multi-agent social deduction games, where final outcomes are noisy and do not explain agent decisions. It introduces an auditable Werewolf framework with external belief states, structured belief-update logs, belief-action deviation evidence, and an offline improvement loop. The paper examines whether LLM agents in social deduction games possess accurate internal beliefs, beyond what public behavior and outcomes reveal. It presents MafiaScope, a non-invasive testbed that privately probes agents after each utterance, scores beliefs against engine ground truth, and supports time-aligned visualization and counterfactual replay. Yixiong Chen, Alan Yuille · 2026-07-12
cs.AIllm agentsoffline preference optimizationexpert trajectoriestool use
The paper addresses limitations of supervised fine-tuning for LLM agents trained from expert trajectories, arguing that imitation does not teach state-conditioned action choice against plausible errors. It proposes Agentic-DPO, an offline preference-optimization method that contrasts expert actions with sampled wrong actions at each state, plus Policy-Preserving Augmentation to separate policy learning from schema variation. Experiments across agent benchmarks show consistent gains over imitation without online rollouts or reward models.
中文摘要
